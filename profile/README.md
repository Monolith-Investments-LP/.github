## The physical bill

Artificial intelligence is discussed as software. It arrives as concrete, copper, turbines, and land — a capital cycle with a physical bill, paid years before the returns are legible.

That bill is the part markets price worst. Software scales without asking permission. Infrastructure asks permission from utilities, regulators, supply chains, and whoever already owns the site. The binding constraint is not intelligence but interconnection, and constraints of that kind resolve on schedules that quarterly attention spans do not reward.

## Why this is a macro question

A buildout of this size has to be financed, and financing is a story about rates, currencies, and sovereign appetite.

| Layer | The question it actually asks |
| --- | --- |
| Financing | Rates, currencies, sovereign appetite |
| Power | A commodity story |
| Fabrication capacity | A geopolitical one |

We carry a discretionary macro book alongside the portfolio because the infrastructure position is, in the end, a wager on how those forces settle.

## How we invest

We hold concentrated positions across the physical and financial layers of the buildout, sized by conviction rather than spread thin for its own sake.

Concentration is only defensible with real understanding, which is why we build our own research and execution stack instead of renting one. It is the same discipline we underwrite, turned on ourselves: operating inside a buildout is the cheapest way to learn where its constraints actually bind.

## Principles

- **Long-term oriented.** We are patient with capital cycles that resolve slowly.
- **Timing is a separate claim.** We stay alert to the difference between a thesis being right and being right on time.
- **Owned end to end.** Monolith is built as one integrated system.
